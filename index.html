About Me

Background

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Aerospace & Mechanical Engineering at the University of Oklahoma. My work lies at the convergence of experimental materials science, electrochemical energy systems, and data-driven modeling.

My current research spans shape memory polymer composites — where I develop ML-assisted models for predicting shape recovery behavior — and lithium-ion / lithium-metal battery systems, including physics-based degradation modeling and model-predictive control strategies.

Previously at the University of North Texas, I worked on next-generation Iron–CO₂ batteries with 2D transition-metal dichalcogenide catalysts and additively manufactured Zn–V₂O₅ batteries, gaining deep expertise in electrochemical characterization and functional material fabrication.

What I Work On

Research Interests

Shape Memory Polymer Composites

Developing ML-assisted prediction models for shape recovery behavior of carbon-fiber reinforced shape memory epoxy composites. Studying thermo-mechanical programming and recovery mechanisms.

Battery Modeling & Control

Physics-based degradation modeling for graphite–silicon composite electrodes. Model Predictive Control for Li-metal batteries with voltage, temperature, and electrolyte constraints.

Physics-Informed Machine Learning

Integrating physics-based priors into machine learning frameworks for predictive modeling of complex material systems and electrochemical processes.

Additive Manufacturing

Fabrication of functional materials through additive manufacturing — including 4D-printed liquid crystal elastomers, Zn–V₂O₅ batteries, and PVDF–MoSe₂ piezoelectric sensors.
